The basics

What is the “stock market”?

A marketplace where people buy and sell shares (ownership in public companies).

CompaniesSell shares to raise money to grow
ExchangesNYSE, Nasdaq, open every business day
InvestorsBuy shares to own part of future profits

Every trade has a buyer and a seller. The price is where they agree.

When the news says “the market”

It's just a stock index: a group of securities tracked together.

What makes it go up or down?

Company profitsGrowing profits push prices up over time. Shrinking profits pull them down.
Interest ratesHigher rates make borrowing cost more and bonds more attractive. Stocks often dip.
The economyJobs, wages, and spending. A strong economy usually means stronger companies.
InflationFast-rising prices squeeze profits and push rates higher.
World eventsElections, wars, pandemics, new laws. Surprises move prices fast.
Investor emotionFear and greed. People often sell in a panic and buy in a frenzy.

Inflation

The quiet money leak

What it is

Prices rise over time. So every dollar buys a little less each year.

Why it matters

Your money has to grow faster than prices, or you go backward. Retirement can last 30 years, and your income has to keep up.

What $100 buys at 2.5% inflation a year

Each bar is 5 more years. In 5 years, $100 buys what $88 buys today.

Diversification

Don't bet it all on one thing

What it means

Spread your money across things that don't move together: stocks and bonds, big and small companies, different industries, the U.S. and the world.

Why it matters
  • One bad pick can't sink the whole plan.
  • A smoother ride, so you stay in when it gets rough.
  • Something is usually working, even in a bad year.

Asset allocation

How you split it

Asset allocation is your mix of stocks, bonds, and cash. Diversification spreads money inside each one.

Stock

You own a slice of a company.

You share in its growth and its drops. More growth over time, bigger swings.

Bond

You lend money to a company or government.

They pay you interest, then pay you back. Steadier, slower growth.

Cash

Savings, money market, CDs.

Safe and ready to use. Often barely keeps up with inflation.
